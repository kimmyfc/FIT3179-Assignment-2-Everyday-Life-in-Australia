01 / HOW WE SPEND OUR TIME

A day is more than
the workday.

Daily averages reveal the quiet weight of personal care, unpaid work and free time. These measures cover the whole population, including people who did not do an activity that day.

02

Who does what?

Daily minutes by sex · total population

Women average 30 more minutes of housework per day than men.

ABS, How Australians Use Their Time, Table 1

★ What we found

  1. Personal care, including sleep, takes the most time: 643 minutes a day, more than ten hours. 01
  2. Free time (304 min) is a bigger daily block than work and study (252 min) once the whole population is averaged. 01
  3. Unpaid work is not shared equally: women average about 30 more minutes of housework a day. 02

↔ How it connects

The five hours of free time are what the rest of this story is about: how much of it goes to sport and activity, shared places, community groups, and whether it leaves people feeling connected.

! Read with care

  • Averages include people who did not do an activity that day, so they are lower than typical time for people who do it.
  • The gap between men and women describes a pattern; it does not explain why it happens.

Libraries bounced back

National public library measures · each indexed to 2019–20 = 100

Visits recovered to 87.6 million in 2023–24 and digital loans grew by 62%, but registered members kept falling.

ALIA & NSLA, Australian Public Library Statistical Report 2023–24, national five-year comparison

08

Who goes to library programs?

Program participants, 2023–24 · column width = state's share, segments = audience

About half of the 7 million program attendances were early-childhood sessions such as story time. Hover to see smaller states.

ALIA & NSLA, Australian Public Library Statistical Report 2023–24, programs by target audience

★ What we found

  1. About 10,000 libraries, community centres and sports centres are mapped, clustered where people live. 06
  2. Library visits fell to about two-thirds of their 2019–20 level, then recovered to 87.6 million; digital loans grew 62%. 07
  3. Registered members fell from 36% to 30% of the population, even as visits came back. 07
  4. Half of library program attendances are early-childhood sessions; VIC, NSW and QLD hold about three-quarters of participants. 08

↔ How it connects

Libraries are used more than membership suggests: people come back for space and programs, especially families with young children. These are the low-cost places where the community ties in the next section can form.

! Read with care

  • OpenStreetMap is edited by volunteers, so some places are missing and coverage is uneven; read the map as a pattern, not a register.
  • The library chart shows indexes (2019–20 = 100), so it compares change, not size.

★ What we found

  2. About 5.0 million people volunteer formally, concentrated in NSW, VIC and QLD. 10 11

! Read with care

  • The hours bands are rounded estimates, so they may not add exactly to the published total.
  • The 2019 and 2025 surveys span the pandemic, but this data cannot explain why participation fell.

! Read with care

  • The waffle rounds to whole squares; the printed value is the survey estimate.
  • The trust bars centre the neutral share, so both sides can be compared.
  • The life satisfaction axis starts at 6 to show differences under one point, and there is no data between survey years.
  • Gen Z was too young to be surveyed in 2010–12.

THE STORY SO FAR

Everyday life is made
of unequal time and
shared places.

Women spend more time on housework, weekly activity varies by place, and libraries have refilled since the pandemic. Millions still attend cultural events and volunteer. Yet formal volunteering has fallen since 2019, and younger Australians feel they belong less than their parents did at the same stage. These datasets describe patterns; they do not explain why those patterns occur.

SOURCES & METHOD

Behind the numbers.

This page combines five sources: the ABS Time Use Survey (2020–21), the ABS General Social Survey (2025 with earlier years), AusPlay state and national tables (Australian Sports Commission), the Australian Public Library Statistical Report 2023–24 (ALIA and National and State Libraries Australia) and the Scanlon Foundation Research Institute's Mapping Social Cohesion 2025. Community places come from OpenStreetMap. State maps use simplified 2021 ABS state and territory boundaries in an Albers equal-area projection; the ACT is shown separately on the activity map because it is small at national scale.

Formal volunteering means unpaid work through an organisation in the last 12 months. Cultural attendance means attendance at selected venues or events in the last 12 months. Daily time is an average across the whole population, including people who did not do the activity. Survey estimates have margins of error, and small states and territories are less precise. Survey populations and years differ, so read each chart within its own caption.
